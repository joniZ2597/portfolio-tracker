## Trust boundary (operator-attested evidence) — Owner ruling G1, 2026-10-07
Two inputs to the writer are operator-attested and are not verified server-side in B1+B3:
- (a) **the Yahoo daily series.** The CLI fetches it and submits it in full; the writer checks schema and consistency (1 to 30000 bars, exact `{ date, high }`, real calendar days, positive finite highs, strictly ascending and unique dates, last bar within 10 days before or 1 day after the server clock) and derives everything else from it, but it does not fetch Yahoo itself. A trimmed or altered series is outside what the writer can detect.
- (b) **the three TradingView readings** `tradingViewHigh`, `tradingViewFirstBarDate` and `tradingViewAdjSetting`. The writer validates their shape and uses them consistently (the match, and the covered / uncovered split by first-bar date) but does not fetch or verify TradingView. A falsified reading, for example an earlier first-bar date that makes an uncovered Yahoo bar look covered, is therefore outside what the writer can detect, exactly as a falsified High would be.
The record keeps both the TradingView readings and the derived bar count and first-bar date as audit evidence. No further live or provider dependency is added in this slice; a server-side provider fetch may be reconsidered with B2 under its own live-call approval, and a future slice with server-side independent verification can tighten the boundary then. Codex G1 stance (round 4): **not raised** — "the brief requires recording the operator's TradingView High and first-bar date, but does not require the writer to independently verify the first-bar date."
